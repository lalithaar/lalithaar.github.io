# Every business doesn’t need a website.

Last week, I was talking to this client who said ‘I need a website’ and I asked ‘why so’. 

They went ‘I want my address online, customers call and ask for them every time. I was thinking of creating a website anyway because well every business has one, so this seemed like a good time.’

‘Where did they find your number from?’

‘From the google business thing, where you enter your business and add number etc, so it shows up to the right when they search in google’

‘Why not add the address there itself?’

‘I tried to, and it indeed has an address - I typed it correctly. But it decided that’s not the correct address and added something else and I was not able to change it. My friends didn’t know how too’

We spent the next few minutes setting it up and google finally showed the correct address.

He was a computer service and repair person, a really good one. I checked his reviews. 

People call him directly not because he has a fancy website, but he has been around for a decade and does what he does really well, there is trust that he will do it right.

He doesn’t need a website, a google profile showing up is enough.

If I need to service my house’s AC, I wouldn’t search on google, read 10 websites of different repair services and then spend weeks thinking ‘mm, who to call’. 

I would just call my neighbor to ask about that one anna who did the service for them.

They don’t need a site either.

But, think of if we are choosing a school or a hospital. If I am asked for a suggestion for my niece, our first options in mind would be the closest ones that everyone uses as landmarks when driving ‘oh, maybe the kottai school near the old bus stand?’

But then, we also look up on google, see the schools that come up - see if they are CBSE or matric or some advanced stuff, how is JEE coaching?

And in case of hospitals, it would be ‘is this doctor reliable? How are the fees? What do other patients say about it online?’

Or we search for home builders, architects, CA’s.

I remember my aunt reading up the blogs for the first time because that was the first time she took out a house loan and she wanted to know what to look for when choosing who will build the house.

There were 2 major ones in her city - and one of them actually had a blog where they shared experiences and how to decide. What she read was about how to know which architect is right and what skills they have.

It wasn’t even an ad, just something she found online that helped her understand it a bit more.

Okay, she felt much more familiar and now she has to call and ask for quotes. 
She had 2 options in hand

The one who’s site she was just reading and know how the price is like, what’s the process and how long it will take. There was a mobile number right there.
A number someone shared through their friend.

Guess who she called? The first one. The point isn’t “Be online or you can’t keep up”. Please don’t believe so.

You have a business for years and more than that, have people’s trust and familiarity that nothing else can match. 

But rather think of it like this, for someone making a big decision for themself or their children, why not have an easier way for them to understand what you do and how you help better?

If you look at businesses in general, everyone sells a product or service and makes money, and profit in return.

But every business has something so unique that no one outside can quite replicate.

My school I studied in had this anthem that we sing of how please lead us to the right path and make us shine, but I know it because I studied for years - and somehow that story is never online— unless you choose to let them know.

So, let a website (if you choose and decide it’s right to have one) — be something that showcases who you are and what’s special about what you do. 

That is what makes it easier for a fellow human to trust that choice over a random stranger’s.

It’s moving from ‘random’ to ‘oh that one’ in a customer's mind. Your website gives a way for you to decide what they remember you for.

In a nutshell,

Category 1: If you have a business that 
people spend time thinking of, 
compare options and then decide which to choose
— your website lets you decide and own what they think of you as.

Category 2: If it’s more of something the people just call without as much stakes, you may not need a website and it’s okay not to have one.

So, don’t let the ‘be digital’ pressure force you to create one.
