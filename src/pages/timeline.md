> If you see a gap month or year between these, it's either I am legally bound to keep it shut, or a personal project or it's just being human and staring at the wall or resting. You know, the latter somehow always satisfies  my criteria for choosing problems perfectly.
 
**June 2025:**

I was working on a frontend design for the first time. The user was someone who just want to get off work and go home, a tired user on friday 6 pm. But usual visual standards are 16px font for base text, the color pairs being actually readable hanging off by dreams and prayers and hoping it works (tho it looks really great when coding it and looks aesthetic and COOL)

I wondered "why not let the user have the option to tap a button and let it change to a higher contrast, font" and just easier for the user to go through the app.

That became my first research work:

[*Beyond Compliance: A User-Autonomy Framework for Inclusive and Customizable Web Accessibility*](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.10324)

Well well, I found out a month or so later about the whole horrible experiences people had with something called an "overlay".[^1] [^2]

[^1]: See [Overlay fact sheet](https://overlayfactsheet.com/en/) if you want to go down that rabbit hole.
[^2]: [Protecting the people you serve from terrible experiences even if it started with a good faith intent.]()

Basically that was the idea I converged to as well. So the implementation part of the paper is to be considered BS, but the underlying principle: "The complaince to make the web usable to people by what law mandates is the floor" still stands and is something I believe to this day. [^3]

How did this end? See [Decemeber 2025]().
