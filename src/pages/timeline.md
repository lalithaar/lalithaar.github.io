It produced [A founder's guide to building on public court data without walking into a wall](/a-founders-guide-to-building-on-public-court-data-without-walking-into-a-wall) (more on that below). 

But the problem fit someone who's more of going from 7-9 (maintaince, uptime and reliability) than 0-1, I am more interested in the upstream of this where we define and calibrate what should be maintained, and what risks are involved, etc. So it was a no.

Now onto that guide, it was more of a quick of risk analysis I did before meeting, but here you go.
This is written from the POV of building using court data — a few things worth knowing going in:

 - Public data isn't automatically free to build on commercially. What matters is what you do with it, not just where it came from.
 - Naming a lawyer or litigant is generally fine. Scoring or ranking them without their input is where actual lawsuits have happened elsewhere.
 - Judge-level analytics — even indirect, inferred patterns — is the one thing that's already led to an outright ban in at least one country.
 - If you ever partner directly with a court on an AI system, current draft rules suggest the court can end up with a perpetual, royalty-free license over what you built — worth knowing before that conversation starts.
 - The same underlying data and analysis can be low-risk or high-risk depending entirely on how it's packaged and shipped, not on the data itself.
