# You don't need an mvp to validate, and you don't (mostly) need to raise to build an mvp

I was talking to someone recently of if I could refer them to investors I may know, and that conversion inspired this. Now, the reason for raise was to build the MVP and yes, this maybe true for fields and like every heuristic ever, this isn't absolute black and white. If you need a deep tech that will show itself in 5 years, then you would need to set down on the funds needed, yes. But in general and most cases, you don't.

**Talk to your ICP (target buyer) first.**

Praise, 'good idea' or any sort of verbal positiveness is not a commitment or validation, them signing a letter of intent or putting down actual money is. The gap between them is far and wide.

**Some good resources:**

Research and understand what works in outreach for your specific ICP, it's wildly different and ever changing. esp. with AI and spams.
Once you are clear on positioning and know how to pitch (this is important, unless you have atleast some level of positioning, this isn't as helpful) - ask Boardy for an intro to your potential buyer.
Don't start with 'hey I am selling you stuff' it gets marked as spam, here's why pt.1 is important. (unless they specifically know you/open to being pitched). 
For how to do 3 right, be pitched by who you would be a target to and see why it worked or didn't. Also, check out MOM test book.

But how will they give me that without me having anything to show, isn't that what mvp is supposed to be?

Create a landing page exactly as if it exists, spinning up a UI that works is both a great exercise in positioning for you and also in seeing what works and didn't. It's so much cheaper in execution with UI as long as you handle the taste (that's the part you can't outsource - AI is terrible at it)
Let's say if what you are building is a platform that automates something, is a marketplace, or anything that keeps replicating a single thing - do the thing yourself manually with some commission - you get real money as validation and battle tested workflows for you to now know what's automatable vs not vs when it should not be

By MVP, it's not 'first full stable version', it's 'version that's barebones and cheap enough for you to build, but shows the value enough for the customer to understand what it means for them.

**Here's what this would mean:**

If you want to build a all in one platform, one stop solution - built a single stop that does one thing really well. Doing that one thing if done right will get you money to build the next thing, a customer base who already trust you, and you will have enough of a base to validate the next idea to add to your stack before building. This is also not a new rule. It's just: do one thing, do it well, go deep before going wide.

None of this is a new idea really, it's the same core business principles applied and used as a heuristic for every aspect of the business. View principles as a heuristic instead of a good to listen to, and it's good.
