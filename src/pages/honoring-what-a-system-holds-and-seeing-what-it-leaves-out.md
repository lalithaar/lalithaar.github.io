# Honoring what a system holds, and seeing what it leaves out


There are wonderful systems and huge contributions in this world, including the ones that are open source. They are wondeful and you can respect and be grateful for all the impact they had but still know the boundaries of what they can hold and cannot. 

Wikipedia is one of the biggest open encyclopedias and I don't have to explain the greatness of it. But it's also about knowing that the policies in place to protect the space also has boundaries that keep some kinds of work and people out (ex. policies reg how they document living person and who has a say in it, etc.). There are many wonderful editors protecting the space for it to be what it is, and there are also wonderful editors who contest the same rules to make the space better. 

Hack club has been one of the best communities and it is no small feat (actually terrific) to have a safe space for people, and that too those under 19, aka the minors and the ones who are one of the most vulnerable population online. The safety is unmatched, but it's also okay to acknowledge that being able to build there assumes you have privileges of time, space, a computer, internet, privacy, and having the mental energy to be able to focus on it. 

The point is not that oh so they are bad or flawed, nope every system that chooses to support some has to choose to calibrate and set where their boundaries are, trying to do everything is how you make it harder to do anything. 

But rather, also knowing that you can hold both respect, graditute along with knowing the boundaries of what they hold, that's how you improve the systems, and importantly - acknowledge the very users who are left out knowing so instead of pretending it's for everyone and making the people left out feel isolated[^3]
