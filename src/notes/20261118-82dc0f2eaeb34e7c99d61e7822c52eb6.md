*Copy of my comments on Free and/or Open Source tools in AI Era as posted on Foss United Forum - https://forum.fossunited.org/t/foss-in-the-post-ai-universe/9644/3?u=lalitha_a_r1*

So glad this discussion was brought up, this is something I have thought quite a lot about both from perspective of someone who builds stuff, but also uses a lot of FOSS tools and have seen people use it different ways.

I do think FOSS tools for developers to build better has reduced a lot in terms of value, pre AI it is either you sitting down and spending weeks building it and you are really glad if someone just has the thing you want. But as a dev, you can whip up the software and tools needed fully customized for you in barely any time compared to the prior.

But I don’t think it’s the case with FOSS for non-technical people, if at all. Outside of tech/AI circle, most people from teachers, carpenters, small business owners,…use AI as a better search engine that they don’t have to scroll many videos and articles filled with ads for. Or if it’s indeed a user who’s pretty AI savvy (not the same as tech folks who are AI ‘savvy’), they still use it for stuff like ‘write me an email’ ‘what does this email say, do I need to do anything’ and from personally having witnessed teachers in govt schools use it, it’s ‘go through this sheets and tell me exactly what I need to do’ (they just receive this huge sheets with thousands of rows for schools across the state and they have to scroll in phone to figure out if their school has any action item at all)

And these are not people who will whip up an app as they want it, (it is really just not something they do, they have their day to day responsibilities that are busy enough) and wouldn’t mind paying a India-scale amount if it reduces their friction - same as software/apps had been years ago.

So I feel like this is an opportunity to focus on the far more underserved non technical people and their frictions - with as you said, really cheap to build from our side, while fully impactful to the user.

But with focus on non technical folks, also comes the responsibility of making it really accessible - far more than how we would document for a technical tool. I feel like more discussions can be on how to make discovery and distribution of such tools and apps possible (an aggregation platform that’s FOSS and also a clear benchmark and test heuristics for what makes something a useful app rather than just AI slop, basically making the definition of AI slop clearer.)

Some immediate ideas I have heard requests from actual teachers/business owners of, tho haven’t had the capacity to design it at the depth needed:

- A spreadsheet app that’s mobile first and just used as a documentation system (the spreadsheet one I talked of is just the way they use to coordinate task, there could be much better UX and sharing mechanisms stripping out all the heaviness usual spreadsheets have that are not needed here)
- A timetable maker that grounds it in how they do it by hand to make class timetables, allot duties to staff, staff’s custom constraints, instead of slapping a constrained solver at it (yep tried it lol)
- A way for elderly people to keep in touch with their family when all they know about smart phones if at all is making a phone call (or mostly just answering them) - as simple as possible UI to help them understand and navigate it on a daily basis
